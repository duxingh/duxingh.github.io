For years, I kept switching among [Bear](https://bear.app/), [Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/) and other note-taking apps.

Every few months, I would convince myself that one of them should become my one and only note-taking app. I'd migrate my notes, redesign my workflow, and promise that *this time* I had finally found the perfect system.

A few months later, I would switch back.

Eventually, I realized something simple:

> **The problem wasn't the tool. The problem was my mind.**
{: .prompt-tip }

Today, I no longer see Bear and Obsidian as competitors. Instead, I use three different tools, each serving a distinct purpose:

* **Bear** for input.
* **Obsidian** for organization.
* **My blog** for output.

Together, they form a complete writing workflow.

## Bear: Where Thinking Happens

Bear is my inbox for ideas.

It opens instantly, stays out of my way, and encourages me to write before I start organizing. Whenever I have a thought, a reading note, a journal entry, or the first draft of an article, Bear is where it goes.

At this stage, I don't worry about folders, backlinks, or permanent structure. Thinking comes first.

Bear's flat design is exactly what makes it so enjoyable to use. Instead of deciding where every note belongs, I simply write. Organization can wait.

## Obsidian: Where Knowledge Lives

Not everything I write deserves to become permanent knowledge. Many notes are temporary. Some ideas turn out to be wrong. Others are useful only once.

Obsidian is reserved for something different: Ideas that are worth keeping for years.

This is where Obsidian truly shines. Backlinks transform isolated notes into a growing network of knowledge. Over time, individual concepts begin to connect naturally, often leading to insights I wouldn't have discovered otherwise.

## My Blog: Where Ideas Meet Readers

A blog is not a public notebook. It is not a dumping ground for unfinished thoughts. A blog exists for readers.

Writing for a blog forces me to think differently. I have to provide context, structure my arguments, tell stories, and explain why an idea matters.

## A One-Way Flow

One principle keeps this system simple: **Information only moves forward.**

```markdown
Bear
   ↓
Obsidian
   ↓
Blog
```

Ideas begin in Bear. The best ideas become lasting knowledge in Obsidian. The most valuable knowledge becomes articles on my blog.

I rarely move information in the opposite direction. This prevents duplication and keeps each tool focused on what it does best.
