[*The Feynman Lectures on Physics*](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/) is based on the undergraduate lectures that Richard Feynman delivered at the California Institute of Technology (Caltech) from 1961 to 1963.

## 1. Atoms in Motion

### 1-1 Introduction

From Feynman's words, it sounds like physical laws are tricks that condense the enormous mass of knowledge about nature.

### 1-2 Matter is made of atoms

If one were to convey a wealth of knowledge about the world in a single sentence, Feynman believed that sentence would be the atomic hypothesis:

> all things are made of atoms—little particles that move around in perpetual motion, attracting each other when they are a little distance apart, but repelling upon being squeezed into one another.

---

Based on the atomic hypothesis, we can explain a lot of phenomena by applying a little imagination and thinking.

A drop of water keeps its volume because the molecules attract each other. We interpret the temperature of the water as the jiggling motion of molecules. If we heat the water, the jiggling motion of the molecules increases; thus, the volume of the water increases. If the heating continues, there comes a time when the pulling between molecules can't hold them together; thus, some molecules fly apart. This is the process of vaporizing.
